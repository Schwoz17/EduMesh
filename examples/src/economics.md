# Economics

## Demand and Supply
Level: SS1–SS2

Demand is the quantity of a good that buyers are willing and able to buy at a given price. The law of demand says that when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls, and when the price falls, the quantity demanded rises, other things being equal. When the price of tomatoes rises in the dry season, families buy fewer baskets.

Supply is the quantity of a good that sellers are willing to offer for sale at a given price. The law of supply says that when the price rises, the quantity supplied rises. When yam prices are high, farmers bring more yams to the market.

Equilibrium price is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. At this price there is no shortage and no surplus.

## Inflation
Level: SS2–SS3

Inflation is a persistent rise in the general level of prices over time. When there is inflation, the same naira buys fewer goods than before, so its purchasing power falls.

Causes include demand-pull inflation, where too much money chases too few goods, and cost-push inflation, where production costs such as fuel and transport rise and sellers pass the cost to buyers.

Effects: people on fixed salaries suffer, savings lose value, and borrowers may gain because they repay with money that is worth less. Government can control inflation by reducing money supply, increasing production and controlling costs.
