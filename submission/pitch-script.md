# EduMesh AI — Pitch Script (about 3½ minutes)

Read at a calm pace, about 140 words a minute. Each section matches a slide in the deck; the deck's speaker notes contain the same points.

---

**[Slide 1 — Cover] (15 s)**

Good day, judges. We are [Team name], and this is **EduMesh AI**: a free AI tutor that works with **no internet and no data**.

**[Slide 2 — The problem] (25 s)**

Picture a student in [town/state] studying for WAEC. To ask a tutor a question tonight, they need three things: a data plan their family can afford, a network that stays up, and a tool that understands *their* syllabus and *their* language. Too often, all three fail. And if that student is blind, most apps don't work for them at all.

**[Slide 3 — Our solution] (20 s)**

EduMesh fixes this with one idea: **open a link once, then learn forever, offline.** The first visit saves the app, the textbooks and a small AI model on the phone. After that, every question costs zero naira of data, even in airplane mode.

**[Slide 4 — How it works] (25 s)**

Phones in Nigeria range from new to very old, so EduMesh picks the best way to answer automatically:
- On a good phone, the AI runs **inside the browser**.
- In a classroom with weak phones, **one laptop** shares its AI over Wi-Fi, with no internet.
- On any phone at all, **Textbook Mode** answers straight from the book.

Every answer starts with the textbook, so it stays on the syllabus.

**[Slide 5 — Features] (25 s)**

Students can learn in **English, Pidgin, or Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo mixed with English**. EduMesh notices which one they use and makes it the default. After every quiz, it **predicts their next score**, puts their **weak topics first**, and explains those more simply. Nothing they type or score ever leaves the phone.

**[Slide 6 — Working prototype] (30 s)** — *switch to the live demo or video here*

This isn't a mock-up. *(Turn on airplane mode.)* The network is off. I ask: "Abeg explain Ohm's law." It answers in Pidgin and shows the textbook page it used. I take a quiz, and my readiness score updates. All of this happens offline.

**[Slide 7 — Accessibility] (20 s)**

For blind students, a voice explains the app the first time it opens. A **double-tap anywhere** turns on Voice Mode: high contrast, large text, and every answer read aloud. Quizzes can be answered by voice. A blind student can start learning **without anyone's help**.

**[Slide 8 — Teachers] (20 s)**

How do lessons stay fresh without internet? Teachers write a lesson and quiz in the app, then share it by **Xender, Bluetooth or WhatsApp**, apps students already use. Students import it, and it works offline immediately.

**[Slide 9 — Proof] (15 s)**

It's built and tested: **6 subjects, 58 exam-style questions, 38 automated tests**, and we've proven it answers with the network cut. We're also honest about limits: small models are weaker than ChatGPT, which is exactly why every answer is grounded in the textbook.

**[Slide 10 — Impact] (20 s)**

EduMesh is **free for students**. Schools reuse a laptop they already own. Partners such as state education boards and NGOs can sponsor lesson packs, hub kits and teacher training. **Our ask:** a one-term pilot in [__] schools in [state].

**[Slide 11 — Close] (10 s)**

A student shouldn't need a data plan to ask a tutor a question. With EduMesh, they need **zero naira of data**. Try it now at [link], with airplane mode on. Thank you.

## Likely judge questions — short answers

- **"How big is the download?"** The app is about 0.2 MB. The optional AI model is 0.3–0.9 GB, downloaded once on Wi-Fi. Weaker phones skip it and use Textbook Mode or the Classroom Hub.
- **"Doesn't a 1B model make mistakes?"** It can. That's why every answer is built from the saved textbook, shows its source, and falls back to the textbook text itself if the model fails.
- **"Is the voice input really offline?"** Read-aloud is fully offline. Voice input is offline in browsers that support on-device recognition; in others, such as Chrome, it may need data. Typing always works, and we say this openly.
- **"How do you get real textbooks in?"** Curriculum files are plain Markdown. Partners or teachers add them as lesson packs; no app update is needed.
- **"What about student data?"** None is collected. There are no accounts, and everything stays on the phone.
- **"Why not just use ChatGPT?"** It needs constant data, doesn't follow the Nigerian syllabus, and isn't built for blind learners or local languages.
