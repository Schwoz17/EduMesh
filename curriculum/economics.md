# Economics

## Scarcity, Choice and Opportunity Cost
Level: SS1

Economics studies how people use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants. Human wants are many, but the resources to satisfy them, such as money, land, time and labour, are limited. This is called scarcity.

Because of scarcity, we must make a choice about which wants to satisfy first. A list of wants arranged in order of importance is called a scale of preference.

Opportunity cost is the alternative forgone, that is, the next best thing you gave up when you made a choice. Example: Ade has ₦2,000. He can buy a textbook or a pair of slippers. If he buys the textbook, the opportunity cost is the slippers. Opportunity cost is the real cost of any choice, not the money spent.

## Demand and Supply
Level: SS1–SS2

Demand is the quantity of a good that buyers are willing and able to buy at a given price at a particular time. The law of demand says that when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls, and when the price falls, the quantity demanded rises, other things being equal. When the price of tomatoes rises in the dry season, families buy fewer baskets.

Supply is the quantity of a good that sellers are willing and able to offer for sale at a given price. The law of supply says that when the price rises, the quantity supplied rises. When yam prices are high, farmers bring more yams to the market.

Equilibrium price is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. At this price there is no shortage and no surplus. If the price is above equilibrium there is a surplus, and if it is below equilibrium there is a shortage.

## Money and Its Functions
Level: SS1–SS2

Money is anything generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services and for settling debts. Before money, people used barter, the direct exchange of goods for goods, such as yams for fish.

Barter had problems. The double coincidence of wants was needed: the fish seller must want yams at the same time the yam seller wants fish. Goods were hard to divide, hard to store and had no common measure of value.

Functions of money: it is a medium of exchange, used to buy and sell; a measure of value or unit of account, so the price of everything can be stated in naira; a store of value, because it can be saved for future use; and a standard of deferred payment, so debts can be paid later.

Qualities of good money: it should be generally acceptable, durable, portable, divisible, homogeneous (all units the same) and difficult to forge.

## Inflation
Level: SS2–SS3

Inflation is a persistent rise in the general level of prices over time. When there is inflation, the same naira buys fewer goods than before, so its purchasing power falls.

Causes include demand-pull inflation, where too much money chases too few goods, and cost-push inflation, where production costs such as fuel and transport rise and sellers pass the cost to buyers.

Effects: people on fixed salaries suffer, savings lose value, and borrowers may gain because they repay with money that is worth less. Government can control inflation by reducing the money supply, increasing production and controlling costs.

## Population
Level: SS2

Population is the total number of people living in a country or area at a particular time. It is measured by a census, a counting of all the people in a country.

Factors that increase population are a high birth rate, a falling death rate due to better health care, and immigration. Factors that reduce population are a high death rate, a low birth rate and emigration.

Optimum population is the population size at which output per head is highest, given the available resources. Over-population is when the population is too large for the resources, leading to unemployment and pressure on schools and hospitals. Under-population is when there are too few people to fully use the resources.

Population structure shows the age and sex distribution. Nigeria has a youthful population, with a large share of people under 15, which means a high dependency ratio.
