# Biology

## Photosynthesis
Level: SS1–SS2

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food (glucose) using carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll. Oxygen is released as a by-product.

Word equation: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, using light energy and chlorophyll. Chemical equation: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.

Photosynthesis takes place mainly in the leaves, inside chloroplasts, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll that traps light energy. Carbon dioxide enters through small pores called stomata, and water comes from the soil through the roots and xylem.

The light stage splits water and releases oxygen. The dark stage uses the energy to turn carbon dioxide into glucose.

Factors affecting photosynthesis are light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature and water supply. This is why a cassava farm planted under heavy shade grows poorly.

The importance of photosynthesis: it produces the food that all living things depend on, and it releases the oxygen we breathe.

## The Cell
Level: SS1

The cell is the basic unit of life. All living things are made of one or more cells.

An animal cell has a cell membrane, cytoplasm and a nucleus. The nucleus controls the activities of the cell and contains genetic material. Mitochondria release energy through respiration.

A plant cell has everything an animal cell has, plus a rigid cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts for photosynthesis and a large central vacuole filled with cell sap.

Differences: plant cells have a cell wall and chloroplasts, but animal cells do not. Plant cells usually have a regular shape while animal cells are irregular. Plant cells store food as starch while animal cells store glycogen.

Levels of organisation: cells form tissues, tissues form organs, organs form systems, and systems form the whole organism.

## Respiration
Level: SS1–SS2

Respiration is the breakdown of food (glucose) in living cells to release energy. It happens in the mitochondria of every living cell, all the time.

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy. It releases a lot of energy.

Anaerobic respiration happens without oxygen. In yeast it produces alcohol and carbon dioxide, which is how palm wine ferments and bread dough rises. In human muscles during hard exercise, such as running a long race, it produces lactic acid, which causes muscle tiredness and cramps.

Respiration is different from breathing. Breathing is the movement of air in and out of the lungs. Respiration is the chemical release of energy inside cells.

## Malaria and Disease Vectors
Level: JSS3–SS2

Malaria is caused by a parasite called Plasmodium. It is spread by the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito, which is the vector.

The cycle: a mosquito bites an infected person and takes in the parasite. The parasite develops in the mosquito, which then passes it to the next person it bites. In humans the parasite multiplies in the liver and then in red blood cells.

Symptoms include high fever, headache, shivering, body pains and loss of appetite. Severe malaria can be deadly, especially for children under five and pregnant women.

Prevention: sleep under insecticide-treated nets, clear stagnant water where mosquitoes breed (like old tyres and blocked gutters), clear bushes around the house, and use insect repellent. Malaria is treated with drugs such as artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT) prescribed at a clinic.

## Ecology and Food Chains
Level: SS1–SS2

Ecology is the study of living things and their environment. An ecosystem is a community of organisms interacting with each other and their non-living surroundings.

A food chain shows how energy passes from one organism to another. It starts with a producer, usually a green plant. Example: grass → grasshopper → lizard → hawk. The grass is the producer, the grasshopper is the primary consumer, the lizard is the secondary consumer and the hawk is the tertiary consumer.

A food web is many food chains linked together. Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi break down dead organisms and return nutrients to the soil.

Energy is lost at each step of a food chain, mostly as heat, so there are fewer organisms at higher levels. This is shown as a pyramid of numbers or a pyramid of energy.
