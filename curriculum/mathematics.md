# Mathematics

## Percentages and Profit and Loss
Level: JSS3–SS1

A percentage is a fraction out of 100. To find 15% of ₦2,000, multiply 2,000 by 15 and divide by 100, which gives ₦300.

Profit happens when the selling price is more than the cost price. Loss happens when the selling price is less than the cost price. Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price. Loss = Cost Price − Selling Price.

Profit percent = (Profit ÷ Cost Price) × 100. Loss percent = (Loss ÷ Cost Price) × 100. We always divide by the cost price, not the selling price.

Example: A trader at Balogun Market buys a bag of rice for ₦40,000 and sells it for ₦46,000. The profit is ₦6,000. The profit percent is (6,000 ÷ 40,000) × 100 = 15%.

Example: A tomato seller in Kano buys a basket for ₦12,000. Some tomatoes spoil and she sells the rest for ₦9,000. The loss is ₦3,000 and the loss percent is (3,000 ÷ 12,000) × 100 = 25%.

## Simple Interest
Level: SS1

Simple interest is the extra money paid for borrowing or earned for saving money over time. The formula is I = (P × R × T) ÷ 100, where P is the principal (the amount borrowed or saved), R is the rate in percent per year, and T is the time in years.

The amount is the principal plus the interest: A = P + I.

Example: Mama Ngozi saves ₦50,000 with a cooperative society that pays 8% simple interest per year. After 3 years the interest is (50,000 × 8 × 3) ÷ 100 = ₦12,000. Her total amount is ₦62,000.

To find the time, rearrange the formula: T = (100 × I) ÷ (P × R). To find the rate: R = (100 × I) ÷ (P × T).

## Linear Equations
Level: JSS2–SS1

A linear equation is an equation where the highest power of the unknown is 1, for example 3x + 5 = 20. To solve it, do the same operation to both sides until the unknown stands alone.

Example: 3x + 5 = 20. Subtract 5 from both sides: 3x = 15. Divide both sides by 3: x = 5. Always check your answer: 3(5) + 5 = 20, which is correct.

Word problem: An okada rider charges a fixed ₦200 plus ₦100 for every kilometre. A trip costs ₦900. How many kilometres? Let the distance be d. Then 200 + 100d = 900, so 100d = 700 and d = 7 kilometres.

When the unknown appears on both sides, collect the unknown terms on one side and the numbers on the other. For 5x − 4 = 2x + 11, subtract 2x from both sides to get 3x − 4 = 11, then 3x = 15 and x = 5.

## Quadratic Equations
Level: SS2–SS3

A quadratic equation has the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a is not zero. It can have two solutions, one repeated solution, or no real solution.

Method 1, factorisation: For x² − 5x + 6 = 0, find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to −5. These are −2 and −3. So (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, which gives x = 2 or x = 3.

Method 2, the quadratic formula: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a. The expression b² − 4ac is called the discriminant. If it is positive there are two real roots, if it is zero there is one repeated root, and if it is negative there are no real roots.

Example: For 2x² + 3x − 2 = 0, a = 2, b = 3 and c = −2. The discriminant is 9 + 16 = 25. So x = (−3 ± 5) ÷ 4, giving x = 0.5 or x = −2.

## Mean, Median and Mode
Level: JSS2–SS2

These are measures of central tendency. They describe the middle or typical value of a set of data.

The mean is the sum of all values divided by the number of values. The scores 6, 8, 5, 9 and 7 have a sum of 35, so the mean is 35 ÷ 5 = 7.

The median is the middle value after arranging the data in order. For 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 the median is 7. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle values. For 4, 6, 8, 10 the median is (6 + 8) ÷ 2 = 7.

The mode is the value that appears most often. In the shoe sizes 40, 42, 42, 43, 42, 41, the mode is 42. A shoe seller in Aba uses the mode to decide which size to stock the most.

## Area and Perimeter
Level: JSS1–JSS3

Perimeter is the total distance around the edge of a shape. Area is the amount of surface the shape covers, measured in square units.

Rectangle: perimeter = 2(length + width), area = length × width. A farm plot 30 m long and 20 m wide needs 2(30 + 20) = 100 m of fencing and covers 600 m².

Triangle: area = ½ × base × height. Circle: circumference = 2πr and area = πr², where r is the radius and π is about 22/7 or 3.142.

Example: A round water tank cover has a radius of 7 m. Its area is (22/7) × 7 × 7 = 154 m².
