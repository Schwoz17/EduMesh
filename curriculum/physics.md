# Physics

## Speed, Velocity and Acceleration
Level: SS1

Speed is the distance travelled per unit time. Speed = distance ÷ time, measured in metres per second (m/s). Speed is a scalar quantity because it has size only.

Velocity is speed in a stated direction, so it is a vector quantity. A danfo bus moving at 15 m/s towards Oshodi has a velocity of 15 m/s in that direction.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. Acceleration = (final velocity − initial velocity) ÷ time, measured in m/s². If a car moves from 0 to 20 m/s in 5 seconds, its acceleration is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 m/s². A negative acceleration is called deceleration or retardation, for example a bus braking at a bus stop.

Equations of uniformly accelerated motion: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², and v² = u² + 2as, where u is initial velocity, v is final velocity, a is acceleration, t is time and s is distance.

## Newton's Laws of Motion
Level: SS1–SS2

First law: a body stays at rest or keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed unless an external force acts on it. This tendency is called inertia. When a bus stops suddenly, passengers jerk forward because their bodies want to keep moving.

Second law: the rate of change of momentum is proportional to the applied force. This gives F = ma, where F is force in newtons (N), m is mass in kilograms and a is acceleration. A force of 10 N on a 2 kg mass gives an acceleration of 5 m/s².

Third law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. When you push off from a canoe, the canoe moves backward. A rocket pushes gas downward and the gas pushes the rocket upward.

Momentum is mass × velocity, measured in kg m/s.

## Work, Energy and Power
Level: SS1–SS2

Work is done when a force moves an object in the direction of the force. Work = force × distance, measured in joules (J). Carrying a 200 N bag of cement up a 3 m staircase requires 200 × 3 = 600 J of work.

Energy is the ability to do work. Kinetic energy is energy of motion: KE = ½mv². Potential energy is energy due to position: PE = mgh, where g is about 10 m/s².

The principle of conservation of energy says energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another. In a hydroelectric dam like Kainji, the potential energy of water becomes kinetic energy, which turns turbines to produce electrical energy.

Power is the rate of doing work. Power = work ÷ time, measured in watts (W). A generator rated 2,000 W can supply 2,000 joules every second.

## Electric Current and Ohm's Law
Level: SS2

Electric current is the flow of electric charge, measured in amperes (A). Potential difference, or voltage, is measured in volts (V). Resistance opposes current and is measured in ohms (Ω).

Ohm's law states that the current through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it, if temperature stays constant. V = IR.

Example: A phone charger supplies 5 V to a circuit with a resistance of 2.5 Ω. The current is I = V ÷ R = 5 ÷ 2.5 = 2 A.

Resistors in series: total resistance R = R1 + R2 + R3. Resistors in parallel: 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3. Houses are wired in parallel so that one bulb going off does not switch off the others.

Electrical power P = IV. Electrical energy used = power × time, which is why NEPA bills are measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh).

## Heat and Temperature
Level: SS1

Temperature measures how hot or cold a body is, in degrees Celsius (°C) or kelvin (K). K = °C + 273. Heat is energy that flows from a hotter body to a colder body.

Heat travels in three ways. Conduction passes heat through solids, like a metal spoon in hot pepper soup becoming hot. Convection moves heat through liquids and gases by currents, like water circulating in a boiling pot. Radiation transfers heat as waves without a medium, like heat from the sun reaching us.

Specific heat capacity is the heat needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K. Heat energy Q = mcΔθ, where m is mass, c is specific heat capacity and Δθ is the temperature change. Water has a high specific heat capacity, which is why clay pots of water stay cool.
